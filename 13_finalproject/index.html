There was a main body with a hole in the top center for the stepper motor and another hole at the top for a magnet. There is also a small hole at the side to insert a set screw to hold the piece in place when attached to the motor. <div style="display:flex; gap:30px;"> <div style="text-align:center;"> <p>Main Body</p> <img src="./mainbody.png" alt="main body" style="width:200px"> </div> <div style="text-align:center;"> <p>Hooks</p> <img src="./hooks.png" alt="hooks" style="width:300px"> </div> <div style="text-align:center;"> <p>Both Parts Together</p> <img src="./assembly.png" alt="assembly file" style="width:300px"> </div> </div> <p> Each individual hook has 4 grooves to hold the necklaces or pieces of jewelry. In different iterations of this piece, I used either 4 or 8 hooks. I also played around with detachable arms or having the arms being apart of the main body. In the end, I went with 8 hooks with the hook arms being a part of the main body as this was better for stability and the aesthetic I was going for. </p> <h4>User Interface</h4> <div class="flexrow"> <video width="640" height="480" controls> <source src="drawingX.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> </div> <p> To get the structure to rotate to a specific hook, I went through different iterations of positioning. I first used an interface with 4 buttons as described in <a href="https://zariaferguson.github.io/PS70ZFerguson/07_outputs/index.html"> Week 7</a>. I then incorporated a rotary encoder and OLED screen that allows the user to scroll through the menu of options and choose their desired hook (<a href="https://zariaferguson.github.io/PS70ZFerguson/10_machine/index.html"> Week 10</a>). My final version of this involved incoporating the stepper motor with the rotary encoder and the OLED screen. When the code runs, a menu of 8 hooks appears on the OLED screen for the user to scroll through by rotating the rotary encoder on the side of the box. When the user clicks the button on the rotary encoder, a screen comes up that reads "Hook [i] Moving..." where [i] would be the number of the hook that was selected. The system rotates to the specific hook. Pressing the button again afterwards allows the system to spin continuously again. <p> In order to spin to the correct hook, the code records the current position and the target position of the selected hook and calculates the difference in order to determine whether to spin forwards or backwards to get to the hook. </p> <p> Here is the code incorporating the rotary encoder, the OLED and the stepper motor for positioning of the hooks: </p> <pre class="pre prettyprint"> <code class="language-cpp code"> #include &lt;Wire.h&gt;
